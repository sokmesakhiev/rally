# Platform-processed payments & refund policy — tickets

Scoped 2026-09-06 after auditing the request against the current codebase and
ABA PayWay's API surface.

**The ask:** move from "attendee pays the organizer directly" to Rally
processing the payment, taking its fee, and passing the rest to the host —
with automated refund policies, defined fee handling, and a dispute workflow.

### Why G and H aren't built

**G needs a number ABA hasn't given us.** The whole ticket is accounting for
the gateway fee — who is debited it on a split, and whether it's reported per
transaction. That's question 5 in the brief's ABA email and it's unanswered.
Rally's own commission (`platform_payments.platform_fee_cents`) is known, but
"platform revenue net of absorbed refund fees" is not computable without the
absorbed fee, and adding a `gateway_fee_cents` column now would be guessing at
semantics that depend entirely on that answer. Its third acceptance criterion —
spotting an organization with an unusual refund rate — *is* computable from
today's `Refund` data and is worth splitting out as its own ticket, since it's
useful under the current direct model too.

**H's own scope check says to confirm before building.** Chargebacks are
largely a card mechanism; KHQR is push-payment and closer to a bank transfer.
If most volume is KHQR, an automated dispute-evidence bundle defends against
something that rarely happens. Confirm the payment-method mix from the existing
`payments.raw_response` data first — that's an afternoon's query, not a
feature — and re-scope H against the answer.

Both are better decided by facts that are one email and one query away than by
building now and reworking later.

Two findings from A already constrain the rest, and are worth reading before
picking up D onwards:

- **A 30-day pre-auth ceiling rules out escrow-until-event.** Registration
  opens months ahead, so a hold placed at registration expires first. The host
  is therefore necessarily paid before the event, which makes Ticket F's
  clawback forced rather than one option among several.
- **`AbaPayway::Client.for_event` inverts rather than adapts.** Today it builds
  a client from the *organization's* credentials. Under the platform model the
  pre-auth is created on Rally's account and the organization becomes a payout
  beneficiary — the method's reason for existing goes away for platform events.

## What changes, and what that costs

Today an attendee's money never touches Rally. `AbaPayway::Client.for_event`
builds a client from the *organization's own* PayWay credentials (#331), so
the funds land in the host's merchant account and Rally's only revenue is the
publish fee (`EventPlanPayment`). That was a deliberate property, not an
accident: it keeps Rally out of the payment path entirely.

Processing payments changes Rally's regulatory posture, not just its code.
**Before any of these tickets start, confirm with ABA and a lawyer familiar
with NBC rules that Rally may collect and split participant funds under its
own merchant account.** Nothing below is worth building if the answer is no,
and the answer is much cheaper to get now than after launch.

### Decisions taken up front

1. **Split at capture — Rally holds no float.** Registration pre-authorises
   the participant; capture uses PayWay's *complete pre-auth with payout* so
   the split happens inside PayWay: the platform fee settles to Rally, the
   remainder to the host. Money never rests in a Rally-controlled balance,
   which keeps the licensing exposure as small as this feature allows.
   The cost is that a refund after capture needs a clawback — see Ticket F.
2. **Rally absorbs the gateway fee on refunds.** A participant refunded under
   policy gets 100% of what the policy says, and the host keeps their revenue
   net of the original fee. The processing fee comes out of platform margin.
   It's the cleanest trust story, and it's a real cost per refund — Ticket G
   makes that cost visible before it becomes a surprise.
3. **Both models coexist.** Events created before this ships keep settling
   directly to the organizer's own credentials. New events use the platform
   flow. Nothing in flight changes mid-event.

### What PayWay gives us

Confirmed against developer.payway.com.kh:

- **Pre-auth** — hold funds without capturing (`pre-auth` endpoints)
- **Complete pre-auth with payout** — capture *and* split in one call
- **Payout API** — with `add a beneficiary to whitelist` and
  `update a beneficiary status`
- **Refund API** — already used by `Refunds::IssueRefund`

The beneficiary whitelist is the constraint that shapes onboarding: a host
cannot receive a payout until ABA has approved them as a beneficiary, and
that approval is not instant. Ticket B is about making that wait visible
rather than a mystery.

> **Unverified in the docs:** the exact request shape for the payout array on
> `pre-auth-completion` isn't rendered on ABA's public page. Ticket A's first
> task is to confirm it in sandbox before anything is built on top of it.

## Ticket A — Pre-auth + split capture against PayWay sandbox

A spike, deliberately, before any modelling. Prove in sandbox that Rally can
pre-authorise a participant, capture with a payout split to a second
merchant, and read both legs back.

Answer these, in writing, in the ticket:

- Exact request/response shape for the payout array on `pre-auth-completion`
- How long a pre-auth can be held before it expires (this sets the ceiling on
  any "hold until the refund window closes" option later)
- Whether KHQR supports pre-auth or only cards — **if KHQR is
  capture-only, this whole design changes**, since KHQR is how most
  participants actually pay
- What the split looks like when the capture amount is lower than the
  authorised amount (partial capture)
- Fee mechanics: who is debited the processing fee, and is it visible per
  transaction

**Acceptance criteria**

- [ ] A sandbox transaction exists showing a two-way split
- [ ] Findings written up; if KHQR can't pre-auth, stop and re-scope

## Ticket B — Host payout onboarding (beneficiary whitelisting)

Hosts need to be an approved PayWay beneficiary before they can be paid.

Add to `Organization`: beneficiary reference, status
(`not_started` / `pending` / `approved` / `rejected`), and the timestamps
around it. Surface it in organization settings (#336) alongside the existing
PayWay fields, and make the state legible — "waiting on ABA, typically N
days" beats a spinner.

**Acceptance criteria**

- [ ] A host can submit beneficiary details and see the status change
- [ ] An organization without an approved beneficiary cannot create an event
      on the platform-processing model — and the error says why
- [ ] Status changes are reflected from ABA, not just set optimistically
- [ ] Specs cover each status transition

## Ticket C — `PlatformPayment`, alongside the existing `Payment`

A new model rather than columns bolted onto `Payment`. The existing one
records "attendee paid the organizer"; this records "attendee paid Rally,
Rally split it". Same table would mean every query about money forever has to
ask which kind it is.

Records the gross amount, the platform fee, the host's net, the pre-auth and
capture identifiers, and the state machine
(`authorized` → `captured` → `refunded` / `expired` / `cancelled`).

`Event` gains a `payment_model` (`direct` / `platform`) set at creation and
immutable afterwards, because changing where the money goes for an event that
already has registrations is not a thing anyone should be able to do.

**Acceptance criteria**

- [ ] Money is stored in integer cents throughout, matching the rest of the app
- [ ] gross = platform_fee + host_net holds as a model-level invariant
- [ ] `payment_model` cannot change once set
- [ ] Existing `Payment` behaviour is untouched

## Ticket D — Refund policy templates and custom rules

Policy lives on the event, chosen at creation from templates or customised.

A tier is a cutoff and a percentage — e.g. 100% up to 7 days before, 50% up
to 48 hours, 0% after. Store as ordered tiers, evaluate against the event's
`start_at`.

Templates ship as named presets (Flexible / Standard / Strict). A host picks
one or edits the tiers.

> Policy is captured **onto the registration at the time of registering**, not
> read live from the event. A host who tightens their policy after someone has
> paid must not retroactively reduce that person's refund entitlement — the
> participant agreed to the terms shown at checkout.

**Acceptance criteria**

- [ ] Tiers are validated as non-overlapping and ordered
- [ ] The applicable refund percentage for any given moment is a pure,
      unit-tested function
- [ ] The policy in force is snapshotted per registration
- [ ] The policy is shown at checkout before payment

## Ticket E — Automated refund execution

A participant cancels; the system computes the entitlement from the
snapshotted policy and issues it without a human deciding.

Extends `Refunds::IssueRefund` rather than replacing it — that service
already handles the gateway/manual split, updates
`Payment#refunded_amount_cents`, cancels the registration, and offers the
freed place to the waitlist. All of that stays.

**Acceptance criteria**

- [ ] Refund amount comes from the snapshotted policy, never recomputed live
- [ ] A 0% tier cancels the registration without a refund, and says so clearly
- [ ] The freed capacity still reaches the waitlist
- [ ] Partial refunds leave the payment in a correct running state

## Ticket F — Clawback when the host has already been paid

The consequence of splitting at capture, and the ticket most likely to be
underestimated.

Money has already reached the host, so a refund has to recover their share.
Order of preference:

1. Net it off the host's next payout
2. If they have no upcoming payouts, carry a negative balance and collect
3. If the balance stays negative past a threshold, escalate to staff

Rally has already paid the participant by this point — recovery is between
Rally and the host. A host with no future events and a negative balance is
the case that decides whether this design is affordable, so model it
explicitly rather than discovering it in production.

**Acceptance criteria**

- [ ] Host balance is a first-class, auditable ledger, not a derived number
- [ ] Negative balances are visible to staff and to the host
- [ ] A refund never fails because the host's balance is empty — the
      participant is made whole regardless
- [ ] Specs cover: netting off, negative carry, and escalation

---

## Ticket G — Fee accounting and reporting

Per the up-front decision, Rally absorbs the gateway fee on refunds. That's
an unbounded cost unless it's measured.

Record per transaction: gross, platform fee earned, gateway fee paid, and —
on refund — the gateway fee absorbed. Surface refund cost in the admin
reports alongside revenue.

**Acceptance criteria**

- [ ] Absorbed fees are queryable by period and by organization
- [ ] Admin reports show platform revenue net of absorbed refund fees
- [ ] An organization with an unusual refund rate is visible

---

## Ticket H — Dispute and chargeback evidence

When a participant disputes, assemble the evidence automatically: the
registration record, the policy they agreed to at checkout (Ticket D's
snapshot), check-in scan timestamps if they attended, and the accepted terms
version (`User#terms_version`, already recorded).

> **Scope check for Ticket A's findings:** chargebacks are largely a *card*
> mechanism. KHQR is push-payment, closer to a bank transfer, and generally
> isn't reversible the same way. If most of your volume is KHQR, this ticket
> matters far less than it appears to — confirm the mix before building it.

**Acceptance criteria**

- [ ] An evidence bundle can be generated for any disputed payment
- [ ] Check-in timestamps are included when present
- [ ] Attaches the exact policy and terms version in force at purchase
- [ ] Staff can review before submission — nothing auto-submits

## Open questions

1. **Platform fee: percentage, flat, or both?** And is it charged on top of
   the ticket price (participant pays more) or deducted from it (host receives
   less)? This is a pricing decision that changes what hosts see.
2. **Does the publish-plan fee survive?** Charging both a publish fee and a
   per-registration commission may be a harder sell than one or the other.
3. **Multi-currency.** Payments are USD today; PayWay has an exchange-rate
   endpoint. Out of scope here, but the ledger in Ticket F should not assume
   a single currency.
4. **Payout timing for the host's share** — immediate at capture, or batched?
   Immediate is simpler; batching reduces clawback exposure in Ticket F.
